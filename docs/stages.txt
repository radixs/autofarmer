-----
Stage 0 Water only, test baseline – Test your baseline water (no soil, just warmed tap water)
What you do:

Fill tank with dechlorinated, conditioned tap water
Let it warm to room temp (19–21°C)
Run filter & light
Take measurements

What to expect:
pH ~ 7.3–7.6 (your water)
GH ~ 14°dH (your hardness ≈ 250 mg/L CaCO₃)
KH ~ 6–8°dH
NO₃⁻ around 1–2
No ammonia, no nitrite

Measure:
pH, GH, KH, NO₃⁻

Move to Stage 1 when:
Water is stable over 2–3 days
No contaminants from tank/glass/etc.
Equipment works and parameters don’t drift

-----
🟢 Stage 1 Add soil, cycle it – Add soil + cap and let the tank “soil-cycle”
What you do:

Add your soil layer
Cap with gravel/sand
Fill slowly
Run filter, no plants yet
Lights OFF except for brief checks (soil tanks bloom algae in early days if lights are on)

What you measure:
pH
NH₄⁺
NH₃ (very low ideally)
NO₂⁻
KH
O₂ is optional

Expect soil behavior:
pH may dip slightly
KH may drop slowly
Ammonium NH₄⁺ may rise a little (this is normal)
Ammonia NH₃ should remain basically 0
Nitrite should remain 0

-----
Move to Stage 2 when:
NO₂⁻ = 0
NH₃ = 0
NH₄⁺ is < 1 mg/L (preferably < 0.3 mg/L)
pH is stable
No foul smell from substrate
This usually takes 3–7 days.

-----
🟢 Stage 2 Add huge mass of plants – Add LOTS of plants (“the jungle stage”)

Plants:
Anubias nana – Very tough, slow-growing rhizome plant. Good for shaded areas, can attach to wood/rock. 3-5 plants
Taxiphyllum ‘Spiky’ (Moss) – Moss variety that gives texture and hiding spots for shrimp/snails. ~30-40% of substrate/wood surface
Limnophila aquatica – Fast-growing stem plant; good for background, helps consume nutrients quickly. 4-6 stems planted in background
Ceratopteris thalicroides – Another mid-to-background plant, grows larger leaves, useful for structure. 2-3 plants (for visual height & shelter)
Riccia fluitans – Floating or attached, good for cover/hiding spots especially for shrimp. Small bunch floating or tied onto rock/wood
~5 plants + moss cover + 4-6 stems + 2-3 background + 1 bunch floating


What you do:

Add many fast growers (hornwort, hygrophila, rotala, vallisneria, limnophila, floating plants)
Keep light at your planned 2000 lux / 8h
Begin minimal fertilization ONLY IF plants pale (usually not needed in Walstad)

What you measure:
NO₃⁻ (plants should eat it fast)
pH
KH
NH₄⁺ (should go down)
NO₂⁻
Check O₂ just once out of curiosity

What to expect:
Nitrates (NO₃⁻) drop near zero in 1–2 weeks
KH may slowly soften over time
pH may trend 6.7–7.2
Plants start pearling slightly during midday
Water becomes visually clearer each day

-----
Move to Stage 3 when:

Plants have visible new growth
NO₂⁻ stays 0
NO₃⁻ stays low but non-zero OR goes to 0 (Walstad normal)
NH₄⁺ near 0
pH stable
No algae bloom, or only mild diatoms
This takes 2–3 weeks, sometimes longer depending on plant mass.

-----
🟢 Stage 3 Add 2–3 tiny fish – Add 2–3 tiny fish
Best choices:
Ember tetra
Least rasbora
Endler male (if you want no babies)
Small white cloud mountain minnows (prefer 19–21°C)
Chili rasbora (if not too cool)

Feeding:
Very tiny amounts.
Your goal is to create just enough waste to “feed” the ecosystem.

What you measure:
NH₃
NH₄⁺
NO₂⁻
NO₃⁻
pH
KH

What to expect:
Plants + soil should instantly digest ammonia/ammonium
Nitrite should stay 0
Nitrate may rise but stay under ~10

MAX is 6–10 small nano fish total

-----
Move to Stage 4 when:

All ammonia tests = 0 (NH₃ + NH₄⁺)
NO₂⁻ = 0
NO₃⁻ < 20

Plants growing stronger
Fish behavior normal
Test through 4 weeks of stability.

🟢 Stage 4 Add snails – Add a snail or two
Best choices:
Nerite snail (Neritina family) – Doesn’t over-replicate too fast, eats algae. 1 - 2
Ramshorn snail (Planorbella spp.) – Good scavenger, reproduces but manageable if feeding is moderate. 1 - 2

What you measure:
Nitrate
KH
pH

Snails introduce minimal bioload.
They're mostly algae cleanup.

-----
Move to Stage 5:
After 1 month stable with fish + snails
No algae explosion
Nitrate still < 20
pH stable
KH not crashing

-----
🟢 Stage 5 Add shrimp – Add SHRIMPS (only when the tank is a jungle)

Neocaridina davidi (for example “Red Cherry” variety) – Very popular, hardy, good for beginners. 10 - 15 Need hiding spots!

This is EXACTLY correct, Boss.
Shrimps require:

✔ Mature biofilm
✔ Stable pH
✔ Zero ammonia
✔ Zero nitrite
✔ Nitrate ideally < 15
✔ Stable GH/KH
✔ Lots of plant cover
✔ Lots of hiding places

Add them when tank is at least 3 months old, ideally 4–5 months.
