Background (fast growers = nitrogen vacuum cleaners) These stabilize the tank by absorbing ammonia/ammonium from the soil.

Limnophila aquatica

Count: 8–12 stems
Planting method:

Plant stems in groups of 3–4, spaced ~4–5 cm apart

Put them along the back wall, especially corners

Expect them to reach the surface in 2–3 weeks
Why: They are extremely fast growers → stabilize your nitrogen cycle quickly.


----
Ceratopteris thalictroides (Water sprite)

Count: 2–3 large plants OR 5 small portions
Planting method:

Plant 1–2 in the back center, leave room—they get big

Let 1 piece float if you want softer light (shrimp love this)
Why: Grows fast, oxygenates, shades algae, produces safe microhabitats.


--------
Midground (structure + shade + shrimp shelter)


Hygrophila polysperma (optional but strongly recommended for speed)

Count: 6–8 stems
Planting method:

Put mid-right or mid-left, in a cluster

Trim aggressively once it hits the surface
Why: The ultimate “trash plant” for beginners—nearly unkillable, stabilizes nutrients.

----
Anubias nana / nana petite

Count: 3–5 rhizomes
Planting method:

Do not bury the rhizome

Attach to wood/rock with thread, glue, or wedge

Place in shaded areas under taller plants
Why: Shade-tolerant, long-lived, shrimp playground.

----
Java Fern (Microsorum pteropus) (optional but fits your style)

Count: 2–3 rhizomes
Planting method:

Tie to hardscape in midground

They grow slowly but fill space attractively
Why: Hardy, zero-maintenance, complements Anubias.


--------
Foreground / Carpet Support

Moss (Taxiphyllum ‘Spiky’ or Vesicularia dubyana)

Count: 2–3 large portions (~8×8 cm each)
Planting method:

Tie moss to stones or wood

Create 3–4 moss islands

Leave some moss near substrate in corners
Why: Shrimp biofilm farm, baby shrimp hideout, safe grazing surface.

----

Riccia fluitans (as floater or tied)

Count: One big handful or two portions
Planting method:

Let some float → reduces light intensity in first months

Tie the rest to small stones with mesh or thread for a “lush carpet patch”
Why: Fast-growing, great for shrimp, soaks ammonium.

Pruning

Prune when:
They reach the surface
They shade the lower leaves
Lower stems start dropping leaves
Growth becomes too dense for water flow
Frequency: every 2–4 weeks in a healthy jungle.

🌿 Floating plants (Riccia, Water sprite)
Prune when:
They block too much light (more than ~50% surface coverage)
They cast heavy shade that slows mid/foreground plants
Frequency: weekly or bi-weekly.

🍃 Rhizome plants (Anubias, Java Fern)
Prune when:
Leaves get algae-covered
Leaves turn yellow or damaged
They grow into each other too much
Frequency: once every 1–2 months.

🌳 Moss
Prune when:
It forms big, dense mats that trap debris
It shades itself or blocks flow
Frequency: every 3–6 weeks.

RULES:
Never remove more than 1/3 of total plant mass at once.

Why?
Plants are the biological filter in a Walstad tank.
Removing too much reduces ammonium uptake and can cause mini-cycles.

For stem plants:
Cut halfway down the stem
Discard the old bottom part if bare
Replant the top part (fresh tip grows fastest)

For floating plants:
Remove 70–90% if needed
Floating stuff grows back super fast, doesn’t shock tank
Just don’t cut everything — leave some.

For moss:
Trim lightly like cutting hair
Don’t take the whole cushion out
