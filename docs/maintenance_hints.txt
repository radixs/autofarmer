Because your volume is small, use a dropper and add a small measurable drop (many conditioners say “1 drop per L” for small volumes).

10–20% water change every 2 weeks, becaue shrimps are very sensitive.

pH
Unit: pH (dimensionless)
Target range: 6.5 – 7.5
Notes:
Walstad tanks often drift slightly acidic due to soil and biological activity.
Consistency is more important than the exact number.
pH may rise during the day (plant photosynthesis) and drop at night—normal.

2. Ammonia (NH₃/NH₄⁺)
Unit: mg/L
Target range: 0 mg/L (always)
Notes:
Plants are the primary ammonia consumers in Walstad tanks—this is a core concept.
Any detectable ammonia indicates imbalance, overfeeding, or insufficient plant mass.

3. Nitrite (NO₂⁻)
Unit: mg/L
Target range: 0 mg/L
Notes:
With a proper soil substrate and heavy planting, nitrite should rarely appear after cycling.

4. Nitrate (NO₃⁻)
Unit: mg/L
Best range: 2 – 20 mg/L
Notes:
In Walstad tanks, nitrate is often near zero because plants consume it quickly.
Zero nitrate is NOT automatically bad; it often means “plants outcompete bacteria,” which is normal.
If fish or shrimp look weak, a small rise (5–10 mg/L) is ideal.

5. General Hardness (GH)
Unit: °dH (degrees of German hardness)
Best range: 4 – 10 °dH
Notes:
Necessary for fish osmoregulation and shrimp molting.
Tap water in much of Poland already falls in this range.

6. Carbonate Hardness (KH)
Unit: °dH
Best range: 3 – 8 °dH
Notes:
Provides pH stability.
Walstad prefers moderate KH: too high suppresses natural pH swings plants use for CO₂ availability; too low causes pH crashes.

7. CO₂ concentration
Unit: mg/L
Natural range (no CO₂ injection): 3 – 10 mg/L
Notes:
The tank relies on fish respiration and soil decomposition for CO₂.
Lower temperature (19–21°C) increases dissolved CO₂ slightly, which is good.

8. Oxygen (O₂)
Unit: mg/L
Healthy range: 6 – 8 mg/L at 19–21°C
Notes:
Cooler water holds more oxygen—nice advantage for your room-temperature setup.
Heavy planting + no surface agitation = stable, high O₂ in daytime; slight drops at night are normal.

9. Phosphate (PO₄³⁻)
Unit: mg/L
Best range: 0.2 – 2.0 mg/L
Notes:
Walstad does not chase phosphate numbers.
Tap water + fish food usually provide enough.

10. Iron (Fe)
Unit: mg/L
Best range: 0.05 – 0.2 mg/L
Notes:
Only measure if you suspect chlorosis (yellowing of young leaves).
Soil substrates already contain plenty of iron—usually not needed.

11. Temperature
Unit: °C
Your target: 19 – 21°C
Notes:
Perfectly fine for most plants and many shrimp/snails.
Fish selection must match (white cloud mountain minnows, danios, some rasboras, etc.).
